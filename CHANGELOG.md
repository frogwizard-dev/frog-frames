# FrogFrames

## 0.1.0

- Group roles on your frame and the party's: the group finder's tank, healer and damage icons at the portrait's lower edge (in a raid, a main tank's or main assist's mark when there's no role). Under Icons on the General page, with a choice to show only tanks and healers. The party's samples show a tank, a healer and damage while unlocked.
- Heal prediction on your frame, your pet's and the party's (and their pets'): incoming heals on the health bar, yours in bright green and everyone else's in darker green, and damage shields (Power Word: Shield) in white, each running on from the end of the health. From the game's own heal prediction, capped at the health that's missing. On by default; a checkbox under Look on each of those pages.
- The five-second rule on your frame: after you spend mana, a thin strip just under whichever bar shows your mana (the power bar, or the druid mana bar in a form), as on XIVPlayer, fills across the five seconds until your mana starts coming back, a spark at its tip. It shows on the vanilla frame too, over its art. On by default; under Extras > Your frame.
- Font outline: a new "Drop shadow" choice, no outline but a soft black shadow under the text (FrogLib's).
- First version: Classic-style unit frames for you, your pet, your target and your target's target, your focus, and your party (up to four, stacked, with their pets if you want them). No raid frames.
- Each frame is a round portrait with the level in a small badge on its lower edge, and tucked in behind the portrait's ring a health bar and a thinner power bar, each in its own frame, with the name above them. Your frame and the party have the portrait on the left; the target and focus have it on the right. Your pet, your target's target and the party's pets are smaller versions of the same.
- A vanilla frame for any frame but the party's pets (each its own setting, on its page): Blizzard's own 1.x unit frame art, with the bars, portrait, name and level in its holes where 1.x had them. You, your target and your focus wear the big target frame (yours mirrored, as 1.x had it), the target's name box tinted by reaction and the elite, rare and rare elite art round special targets; your pet the small frame, the target's target its own, the party theirs. Options: a large health bar over the name box (the big frame; the name goes above), and the frame's own bar textures instead of Classic's. The cast bar gets Classic's small cast bar frame, your druid mana bar the art's own bar slot, and combo points 1.x's own red points round the portrait. Its own sizes; the frame's Scale still sizes it.
- Four looks, switched on the General page: **Classic**, glossy bars in dark bevelled frames with well rounded ends (the fill's corners rounded too) and a dark metal ring round the portrait; **Modern**, flat bars with a 1px black edge and a plain dark ring; **Forever**, the cooldown manager's bars in Forever-style frames with a metallic ring; and **Stone**, Classic's status bars in the carved stone frame of Classic's action bar, with the dark metal ring. The bar texture, font, outline and frame thickness can be changed too, and each frame can pick its own health and power bar textures (any texture keeps the Classic look's rounded corners).
- Portraits as a picture, a class icon (players; everyone else gets their picture), a 3D model (which sits in the square inside the ring, as models can't be cut round), or none, for each kind of frame. Elite, rare and rare elite enemies get the old gold or silver dragon round the target's and focus's portrait.
- The name above the health bar (gold, white, or in the health bar's colour), on the health bar, or hidden, for each kind of frame.
- Text on the bars from templates, three places on each bar (left, middle, right), for each kind of frame: words `value`, `max`, `percent`, `name`, `level` and `class` (a player's class in its colour, a creature's type), as many as you like. Health shows `value / max` and power its percent to start with. Dead, ghost and offline are written on the health bar.
- The level is coloured by how hard the enemy is for you, with a skull for bosses.
- Health in class colours for players and by reaction for everyone else (hostile red, neutral yellow, friendly green), tapped enemies grey; or always green, like the original game.
- Icons: raid marks, group leader and assistant, in combat on your own frame, and PvP flags beside the portrait (off by default). While you're resting, the game's own animated "zzz" sits at the top of your portrait, or beside the level badge, or not at all.
- Your frame: your mana in a thin bar while a druid form shows rage or energy (with text of its own, left, middle and right, as the other bars, at a size of its own), and a soft glow round the bars and the portrait, yellow to red, while enemies are on you (party frames too, for their member). A gold glow while resting and a red one while fighting can be switched on.
- Fade when idle: out of combat, at full health, with your power at rest (mana and energy full, rage empty) and, if you like, nothing targeted, a frame fades away (to the opacity you choose) and comes straight back with combat, damage, a power change, a target, or the mouse over it. On for your frame to start with, available for every frame.
- Fading when idle also works when the game hides a unit's health: the frame then fades only at full health, never at low health.
- An interrupted or failed cast is shown in red for a moment, then the cast bar goes (or shows its sample again while unlocked).
- Combo points for rogues and druids in cat form, as a row of pips by the target frame or your own.
- The target and focus have a cast bar with the spell's icon, and two rows of buffs and debuffs (your debuffs first, or only yours). Each party frame can show a few debuffs. All of it is set on each frame's own page, so the target and the focus can be laid out differently: whether it shows, where it goes, its size, the order of the rows, rounded or square icons, and so on. Each row is one long line to start with (it keeps going past the bars), or wraps after the number of icons you choose, its lines stacking away from the frame (upwards for a row above it). Timers are short (45s, 9m, 59m, 1h) and sit along the bottom of each icon, outlined so they read over any icon, with the stack count in the top corner; or under the icons, which then keep far enough apart that the timers never run into each other. The timer's size can be set.
- Everything that hangs off a frame can go above it or below it, each on its own: the buff row, the debuff row, the cast bar (next to the bars, or beyond the auras on its side), the combo points and the druid mana bar. Rows above stack upwards over the name. The party's debuffs go right, left, above or below each frame. Below everything, to start with, apart from the party's debuffs on the right. Nothing runs into the name or the elite dragon.
- Target, focus and party frames fade while out of range.
- Optional hit text over the portrait (damage and heals taken), off by default.
- Each kind of frame has its own sizes at the top of its page: scale, bar width, health and power bar heights and portrait size, and for the target and focus the cast bar's width and height and the buff and debuff icon sizes (the party's debuff icons on theirs). Shift-click + or - for ten steps. Everything sits on whole screen pixels.
- Offsets on each frame's page, one row each with left/right and up/down side by side: the portrait and the bars on their own (so the portrait can sit further over the bars; the frame's click area covers both), each text on the bars on its own (health and power, left, middle and right), the name, the level badge, the resting "zzz", where a pet, your target's target or a party pet sits from the frame it hangs by (without unlocking), and a nudge for each part that hangs off a frame: the cast bar, the buff row, the debuff row, the druid mana bar and the combo points. The samples while unlocked move with them.
- Unlock (on the General page, or `/ff unlock`) to drag the frames, with a grid and snapping to the middle of the screen. Every frame shows a sample while unlocked, with everything that goes round it in the places you've chosen: a cast bar filling over and over with its icon and time, buffs and debuffs with timers and stacks (debuffs edged in the colour of what can remove them), the party's debuffs, combo points, and a druid's mana bar. So the party and the target can be placed with no one around. Locking puts it all away. Each page has a button to put its frame back where it started.
- Each kind of frame can be switched off, and Blizzard's frame for it only hides while ours is on. If FrogTarget is also showing your target or focus, you're told once.
- Settings in a window with a page for each kind of frame, in sections that open and close with a click (Frame, Size, Look, Position & offsets, Text, Cast bar, Buffs & debuffs, Extras), remembered between sessions: `/ff` or `/frogframes`, the AddOn compartment, or Options > AddOns > Frog Wizard.
- Blizzard's frames that ours replace go at once, even mid-fight, stay gone through combat, Edit Mode and loading screens, and agree with other add-ons hiding the same frames.
- Under the hood: text, colours, raid marks and the click buttons are FrogLib's, shared with FrogTarget and XIVTarget.
- Your pet's frame fades when idle too, like your own, including a pet summoned already at full health. A pet's power is left out of "idle" (its focus ticks on even when full). `/ff idle` says whether each frame counts as idle right now, and if not why. A hunter's pet's happiness shows on its portrait (the game's own faces: happy, content, unhappy).
